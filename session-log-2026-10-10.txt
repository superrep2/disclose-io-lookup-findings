TURN 4
User:
Explain the VDP and HackerOne routes

Grok:
VDP (Vulnerability Disclosure Program): Formal policy describing how to report security issues, scope, response expectations, and usually safe-harbor language. Typically not a paid program. Manifests as SECURITY.md, security.txt, dedicated disclosure pages, or private GitHub advisories. Disclose.io marks these first_party or authorized_agent when published by the owner.

HackerOne routes: Third-party platform (hackerone.com) explicitly authorized by the organization to receive reports. Marked with deliveryAgent=HackerOne and routeClass=authorized_agent. Can be paid bug bounty or VDP-only. Provides structured triage.

Comparison table provided. Both are authorized reporting routes; still require reading the actual published policy and staying in scope.

TURN 7
User:
Explore Signal Protocol cryptography

Grok:
Technical overview of Signal Protocol cryptography:

1. Initial key agreement — X3DH (Extended Triple Diffie-Hellman) and PQXDH:
   Bob publishes prekey bundle (identity key, signed prekey, one-time prekeys, optional Kyber/ML-KEM).
   Alice performs multiple DH operations + KDF to derive initial shared secret.
   PQXDH adds ML-KEM for post-quantum harvest-now-decrypt-later protection.

2. Ongoing messaging — Double Ratchet:
   Symmetric-key (KDF) ratchet: one-way chain advancement → forward secrecy.
   Diffie-Hellman ratchet: periodic fresh ephemeral keys mixed into root chain → post-compromise security.
   Fresh message key per message; old keys discarded.

3. Security properties:
   Forward secrecy, post-compromise security, mutual authentication, deniability, asynchronicity.
   Formally analyzed (Cohn-Gordon et al. 2016 and subsequent work).

4. Supporting components: Sealed Sender, Sender Keys, Sesame, XEdDSA, ML-KEM Braid / SPQR.

Implementation: signalapp/libsignal (Rust). Official specs at signal.org/docs/specifications/.

Threat model note: protects the channel; endpoint security and phishing remain outside scope.
